# Reference Library

Design exemplars to draw from. For each one: what it does, the specific
characteristics you can borrow, and when it fits. Pick one or two per project and borrow
*principles and techniques*. **Never copy a brand's identity, logo, or proprietary
fonts, and never make output that could be mistaken for that company's material.**

Contents:
1. Product & brand exemplars (Stripe, Linear, Apple, Vercel, Notion, Figma, Airbnb)
2. Movements & schools (Swiss/International, Bauhaus, Vignelli, Dieter Rams, editorial)
3. Data & information design (Tufte, the Economist/FT style, Few)
4. Matching exemplars to tone
5. Type pairings (freely available fonts)
6. Palettes

## 1. Product & brand exemplars

### Stripe: *clarity with warmth*
- **Traits:** Very clean layouts with rich, carefully graded color fields; crisp
  sans-serif type with tight display tracking; documentation-grade clarity; diagrams
  and illustrations that explain instead of decorate.
- **Borrow:** The contrast between restrained content areas and one expressive color
  moment (the gradient hero); generous whitespace; precise alignment; code/data in a
  refined mono; explanatory diagrams with thin strokes and one accent.
- **Fits:** Fintech, B2B products, developer-facing reports, anything that must feel
  trustworthy *and* modern.

### Linear: *precision and restraint in the dark*
- **Traits:** Dark, near-black surfaces with very subtle tonal layering; hairline borders
  and inner highlights; small, dense, carefully weighted type; muted palette with sparing
  accent use; quick, subtle motion.
- **Borrow:** Tonal surface stacking instead of shadows; `rgba(255,255,255,.06–.1)`
  hairlines; secondary text in low-contrast grays; tight 13–14px UI type; keyboard-first
  polish; very little color.
- **Fits:** Dashboards, internal tools, product mockups, engineering reports, anything
  "pro tool".

### Apple: *product as hero, radical reduction*
- **Traits:** Enormous headlines with short copy; the object or number isolated on a
  vast field; very large whitespace; one message per screen; cinematic pacing between
  sections; system-font-like grotesque type.
- **Borrow:** The "one thing per view" discipline; giant type contrasts (80–120px
  headline against 17–21px body); full-bleed pacing; letting a single image or number
  carry a slide.
- **Fits:** Keynote-style presentations, launches, landing pages, stat-led storytelling.

### Vercel: *monochrome engineering*
- **Traits:** Black-and-white with almost no color; geometric grotesque plus mono;
  grid-line backgrounds; sharp contrast; technical diagrams.
- **Borrow:** Pure-neutral palettes where the accent is sometimes just black; visible
  grid lines as texture; mono labels; high-contrast edges.
- **Fits:** Developer tools, technical docs, minimalist portfolios.

### Notion: *friendly editorial minimalism*
- **Traits:** Warm off-white, black text, serif accents, hand-drawn illustration
  moments, generous reading measure.
- **Borrow:** Document-first calm; warm neutrals; a single playful element in a
  restrained whole.
- **Fits:** Docs, knowledge bases, internal reports, approachable one-pagers.

### Figma / Airbnb: *confident color, human warmth*
- **Traits:** Bold, saturated color used in blocks; rounded, friendly geometry;
  photography and illustration with people.
- **Borrow:** Color-blocking for section identity; rounded but consistent radii;
  warmth through imagery and copy tone.
- **Fits:** Consumer-facing decks, marketing pages, community reports.

## 2. Movements & schools

### Swiss / International Typographic Style (Müller-Brockmann, Hofmann, Ruder)
- **Principles:** Mathematical grids; asymmetric layouts; flush-left, ragged-right text;
  sans-serif type (Akzidenz, Helvetica, Univers); objective photography; hierarchy by
  size and position rather than ornament; generous negative space.
- **Borrow:** A strict modular grid; a single typeface in 2–3 weights; big-small scale
  contrast; flush-left everything; rules to structure space; red/black/white palettes.
- **Fits:** Nearly everything. It's the safest foundation for professional work.
  Especially reports, dashboards, and posters.

### Bauhaus (Gropius, Moholy-Nagy, Bayer, Albers)
- **Principles:** Form follows function; geometric primitives (circle, square,
  triangle); primary colors against black and white; unification of art and industry;
  asymmetry; type as image.
- **Borrow:** Bold geometric shapes as compositional anchors; a primary-color accent
  set (red, yellow, blue) used sparingly on a neutral field; strong diagonals; lowercase
  geometric sans.
- **Fits:** Title slides, covers, posters, event pages, creative portfolios, anything
  that needs graphic boldness with rigor.

### Massimo Vignelli (NYC Subway map, Unimark, *The Vignelli Canon*)
- **Principles:** Discipline; a very small set of typefaces; grids as the structure of
  thought; "if you do it right, it will last forever"; semantics → syntactics → pragmatics.
- **Borrow:** Restricting yourself to one or two typefaces in a few sizes; bold black
  bands and rules; strict consistency across a system.
- **Fits:** Systems of pages or slides, wayfinding-style navigation, corporate reports.

### Dieter Rams (Braun): "Less, but better"
- **Ten principles, condensed:** innovative, useful, aesthetic, understandable,
  unobtrusive, honest, long-lasting, thorough to the last detail, environmentally
  friendly, as little design as possible.
- **Borrow:** The restraint test: every element must be useful; muted neutrals with
  one functional accent (Braun's orange/green); details finished to the edge.
- **Fits:** Use it as a *filter* on any project during the restraint pass.

### Editorial / magazine design (NYT Magazine, Monocle, Bloomberg Businessweek)
- **Principles:** Expressive display serifs; dramatic scale changes; pull quotes;
  multi-column text with marginalia; strong art direction; covers as statements.
- **Borrow:** Oversized serif headlines; drop caps (sparingly); deck/standfirst
  under headlines; bylines and metadata in small caps; pacing between dense and open pages.
- **Fits:** Reports, case studies, narrative presentations, portfolios, long-form pages.

## 3. Data & information design

### Edward Tufte
- **Principles:** Maximize data-ink ratio; remove chartjunk; small multiples; sparklines;
  integrate text and graphics; show comparisons; show causality; respect the viewer's
  intelligence with dense but clear displays.
- **Borrow:** Minimal axes; direct labels; sparklines in text and tables; marginal notes;
  high information density with low noise.

### The Economist / Financial Times charts
- **Principles:** Headline states the finding; subtitle gives metric + unit; source line
  at bottom; one highlight color; strong typographic consistency; horizontal gridlines only.
- **Borrow:** The whole chart anatomy: title → subtitle → chart → source. The
  "highlight one, gray the rest" approach. Left-aligned titles.

### Stephen Few (dashboard design)
- **Principles:** Dashboards should be glanceable on one screen; avoid decoration; use
  bullet graphs instead of gauges; group related metrics; muted palette with alert color
  only for exceptions.
- **Borrow:** Bullet graphs; muted defaults with exception-only color; dense but organized
  layout.

## 4. Matching exemplars to tone

| Tone adjectives | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Calm, precise, trustworthy | Swiss + Stripe |
| Technical, focused, pro | Linear + Vercel |
| Bold, confident, keynote | Apple + color-blocking |
| Warm, human, approachable | Notion + Airbnb |
| Premium, editorial, narrative | Editorial magazine + Vignelli |
| Graphic, artistic, energetic | Bauhaus + Swiss poster |
| Analytical, dense, rigorous | Tufte + Economist/FT |
| Minimal, timeless, product-y | Dieter Rams + Apple |

## 5. Type pairings

All freely available (Google Fonts or open source). Choose by tone.

| Pairing | Display | Body | Data/mono | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Modern precise** | Inter Tight (600–700) | Inter (400/500) | JetBrains Mono | Stripe/Linear-like clarity |
| **Swiss** | Inter or Helvetica-like grotesk, 1 family only | same | — | Rigorous, neutral |
| **Technical** | Space Grotesk | IBM Plex Sans | IBM Plex Mono | Engineering, dev tools |
| **Editorial** | Fraunces or Playfair Display | Source Serif 4 or Inter | — | Premium, narrative |
| **Refined editorial** | Instrument Serif | Instrument Sans | — | Contemporary, elegant |
| **Warm humanist** | DM Serif Display | DM Sans | DM Mono | Friendly, approachable |
| **Geometric bold** | Manrope (800) or Sora | Manrope (400) | — | Startup, confident |
| **Bauhaus graphic** | Syne or Archivo Black | Archivo | — | Artistic, poster-like |
| **Classic report** | Libre Caslon Display | Libre Franklin | — | Institutional, trustworthy |
| **Data-forward** | IBM Plex Sans (600) | IBM Plex Sans | IBM Plex Mono | Dashboards, finance |

For .pptx/.docx/.xlsx files where custom fonts may not be embedded, pick from
system-safe fonts that still look considered: **Aptos, Segoe UI, Calibri Light (display
only), Georgia, Cambria, Consolas**. Get the craft from scale, weight, and spacing.

**Chart series colors** (when more than one series is truly needed), in order: accent,
then a neutral-700, then a desaturated complementary hue, then neutral-400. Beyond 4
series, consider small multiples instead.

**Semantic colors (muted):** success #1F7A4D, warning #B7791F, danger #C53030, info
#2B6CB0. For backgrounds use these at ~10% alpha.
